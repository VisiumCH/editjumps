# §4.2's cells, on a reference disjoint from the editor's training pairs

Six methods x two families = twelve table rows, in eight JSON/FASTA pairs: the two model-free
baselines (`random_mutations`, `random_homolog_pairing`) are nested inside the editor's own file,
which is where `generation_eval` emits them. Every file here is one cell of one table, and the
reason they are in their own directory is that **they are only a table because they share a
frame**.

## Disjoint Evaluation Protocol

To prevent train-test contamination, evaluation sequences must not overlap with training pairs:

`--disjoint-from-pairs data/pretrain/oas_homolog_pairs.tsv.gz` draws templates and holdout references from family members absent from the training pair corpus via `editjumps.core.family_split.disjoint_members`. Across de-duplicated populations, 18,495 of Ty1's 38,553 members and 21,015 of HER2-VH's 42,839 members are disjoint from the pair corpus.

Every evaluation artifact records `reference_in_training_pairs = 0`, verifying the absence of training leakage.

## The frame

| | |
| --- | --- |
| `n_templates` | 20 |
| `n_variants_per_template` | 20 (400 generated sequences per cell) |
| `holdout_size` | 200 (the scoring reference) |
| `ceiling_n` | 300 (real homologs behind the agreement ceiling) |
| `seed` | 0 |
| mutation budget | matched per §4.2 to that family's editor run: **5** on Ty1, **4** on HER2-VH — the six mask-and-infill cells only; see below |
| `budget_mode` | `realised` (top up until the budget has actually mutated) |

The budget is the one row of that table which is **not** a property of the frame. It is a setting
the six mask-and-infill cells accept and the other rows have no counterpart for: the editor spends
its own clock, and random homolog pairing is exempted by §4.2 outright. Everything above the budget
rows —
the partition, the reference, the ceiling, the seed — is shared by every cell here, which is what
makes them one table.

None of these is cosmetic. Two of Appendix B's set-level metrics — the BLOSUM-smoothed composition
KL and the spectrum-kernel MMD — move with *which* natural sequences are in the reference and *how
many* there are. A cell drawn at a different holdout size or seed is not comparable and must not go
in the table — not because the effect is larger than the between-method spread (measured, it is not:
reference 200 against 800 moves MMD 0.050 on Ty1 and 0.167 on HER2-VH, against method spreads of
0.809 and 0.904 at fixed reference) but because two numbers on different references are not
measuring one quantity. `test_the_disjoint_table_cells_all_share_one_frame` asserts all of it.

Each family's cells also agree on the *alignment frame* — L=119 for Ty1, L=118 for HER2-VH — which
is the first template of the split. Each evaluator computes that independently, so one width per
family is evidence that they all drew the same partition and not merely the same counts. The
positional metrics (`entropy_delta`, the two agreements, `js_divergence_positional`) live in that
frame and are comparable within a family only.

Reproduce the editor's two cells — and the two model-free baselines nested inside them — with
`make disjoint-editor`, which has to go first because `disjoint-baselines` matches its mutation
budget to those files. Then `make disjoint-baselines` for the other six model-based cells —
EvoDiff-MSA and the two evotuned baselines, on both families. Both targets carry the frame
in the Makefile rather than leaving it to whoever types the command; `disjoint-editor`'s comment
names the artefact field each of its flags was read off, and
`test_the_disjoint_editor_target_reproduces_the_committed_editor_cells` asserts they still agree.

## Evaluation Results

The confidence interval on edits/seq is computed via a 20,000-draw percentile bootstrap across the 20 templates generated by `editjumps consolidate`. Model-free baseline statistics are calculated per sequence and averaged across templates.

Three things to read carefully before reading anything into a difference.

**The budget is matched in intent, not in fact.** Only the forced evotuned baseline actually spends
it (5.00 and 4.00); EvoDiff-MSA lands at 2.96 and 2.61 even with top-up, because a masked model
refilling a conserved framework column returns the residue that was already there and the top-up
cap is eight rounds. A row that mutates less is closer to its template on every distance to the
natural holdout **for that reason alone**, which is exactly what "matching the expected number of
mutations per sequence" exists to prevent. Compare a row's metrics against its own realised count
(`mutation_budget.mean_realised`), not against the target.

**Random homolog pairing is not competing.** §4.2 exempts it from the matched budget — it swaps in
a different natural sequence, so it is 23 edits away and wins the KL and MMD by being natural. It
is the ceiling of the distributional metrics, not a baseline the editor should beat.

**The intervals overlap almost everywhere.** On edits/seq the editor's interval covers both
evotuned baselines on **both** families (3.83 and 4.00 sit inside HER2-VH's [3.56, 4.78]) and random
mutations on both. Three of the four columns
(pooled diversity, MMD, composition KL) carry no interval in this table at all — the sibling FASTAs
are here so they can be given one, and until they are, differences of a few percent between rows are
not readable.

## Reading a cell

- `defined_by_the_paper` — the metrics §4.2/Appendix B define.
- `our_interpretation` — metrics the paper plots but never defines; our reading, in the same frame.
- `baselines` — `random_mutations` and `random_homolog_pairing`, computed inside the editor's own
  run against the same reference, sized to its own realised edit count. Across both families they
  are four of the twelve EvoFlows-comparison rows. The other six files carry **no `baselines` key at
  all** — absent, not empty, so `report["baselines"]` raises rather than returning `{}`: a run scores its
  own pair only when asked to, and a pair sized to a *different* method's realised count would be
  the wrong number to put in a table beside the editor's. `consolidate.DISJOINT` draws
  `random mutations` and `random pairing` from the editor's cells only.
- the sibling `.fasta` — the generated sequences, template-major **within each named set**: entry
  `i` of a set came from template `i // n_variants_per_template`. The editor's two FASTAs hold
  **1,200** records in three 400-blocks (`model_*`, `random_mutations_*`,
  `random_homolog_pairing_*`), so the rule applies per block and not across the file; the other six
  hold 400. Split on the header prefix before taking any statistic.

**Intervals.** Resample TEMPLATES, not sequences. The centre of every distance is a mean over
templates, and the twenty variants of one template are correlated by construction; a
sequence-level bootstrap returns an interval about 2.5x too narrow. Every cell carries
`levenshtein_to_template_per_template` for exactly this, and the sibling FASTA lets the set-level
metrics (pooled pairwise Levenshtein, spectrum MMD, composition KL) be resampled by template block
from the same artefact the centre came from. Each file says so in `reading_notes.bootstrap_unit`.

**Diversity.** Use `pairwise_levenshtein_pooled`. `pairwise_levenshtein` is the WITHIN-template
mean, kept for continuity with earlier runs; the two are different quantities and mixing them
across rows compares different things.

## Provenance

| file | method | model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `editor-{ty1,her2vh}` | edit flows (ours) + `random_mutations` + `random_homolog_pairing` | `data/pretrain/eval_B_stock_appA`, clock 40 |
| `evodiff-msa-{ty1,her2vh}` | EvoDiff-MSA, `--mode inpaint` | evodiff 1.1.2 `msa-oa-dm-maxsub`, their released weights |
| `evotune-{ty1,her2vh}` | evotuned PLM | `evotune-dj-{ty1,her2vh}` |
| `evotune-forced-{ty1,her2vh}` | evotuned PLM, forced substitutions | `evotune-dj-{ty1,her2vh}` |

The evotuned trunks are the ones adapted on the **same disjoint population**
(`gs://$DVC_BUCKET/models/esm2_evotuned/evotune-dj-{ty1,her2vh}/`, each shipping
the `evotune_family.{train,val}.txt.gz` the baseline reads for its entropy profile). The default
trunks cannot be used here: their corpora hold 194 of Ty1's 200 disjoint reference sequences and 191
of HER2-VH's, plus 19 of the 20 templates in each, and `evotune_baseline`'s leak assertion refuses
them — correctly. Each disjoint corpus was checked to be a subset of that split's pool, so the
assertion passes on its merits rather than by being weakened.

EvoDiff-MSA ran on CPU (`--device cpu`, which is also what `--device auto` resolves to off a GPU);
the evotuned baselines and the editor ran on this machine's MPS. Both EvoDiff cells were produced by
`--generate-only` shards over one shared per-template cache and scored in a single unsharded pass,
which is bit-identical to one long run: every draw is seeded from the template index rather than
from iteration order.

## Not in these files

`esm2_pseudo_log_likelihood` (Appendix B.2) is empty in every cell — none of these runs passed
`--pll-model`. It needs a stock ESM-2 forward pass per sequence and is a separate comparison from
the cells above.
